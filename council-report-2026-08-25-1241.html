The Question

"Kalau anda di posisi saya, apa yang anda akan lakukan?" — Solo frontend dev Indonesia, butuh income, prototype SliceUI belum deploy/nol user. Council #1 sudah merekomendasikan jasa non-coder; lalu reverse-engineering repo abi/screenshot-to-code menemukan "gap" idiomatic component code. Opsi: (A) jasa murni, (B) dev tool murni, (C) hibrida bertahap. Belum bisa memutuskan.

The One Thing to Do First

Build satu demo page dengan tool-nya hari ini — sebuah bakery, dari awal sampai akhir — dan kirim 10 DM WhatsApp/Instagram pertama yang menunjuk ke demo itu dalam 48 jam. Deploy, demo ke-2 & ke-3, dan situs QRIS boleh menyusul 1–2 hari. Aksi pertama yang mendekati rupiah adalah calon klien yang melihat before/after. Semua yang lain itu persiapan — dan persiapan tidak membayar.

The Recommendation — "C yang Dikoreksi"

Service = mesin kas (survival). Tool = hipotesis yang diuji terpisah. Satu-satunya jembatan = kamu dogfood tool-nya di pekerjaan bayaranmu sendiri.

Minggu 1–8: aturan 4 hari klien / 1 hari tool — tapi agenda hari tool-nya ditentukan oleh pekerjaanmu sendiri, bukan wishful thinking klien. Bangun hanya yang terukur memangkas waktu delivery-mu. Track: jam tersimpan per halaman, siklus revisi, permintaan klien. Nol klien di hari ke-30 = perbaiki offer/channel (bunuh motion penjualan, bukan tool-nya). Revenue di bawah survival di hari ke-90 = repricing atau matikan service-nya.

Minggu 8–12: porting 3 teknik MIT (self-check loop, design-system block, targeted edits) dalam budget 1 hari/minggu. Lalu launch gratis — Show HN, r/webdev, X, komunitas dev Indonesia — sasar eksplisit agensi & freelancer slicing, bukan kafe, bukan pelanggan v0. Biayanya minggu part-time, bukan 6 bulan.

Launch gratis itu vonis untuk teori gap. Nol pull dalam 2–4 minggu: visi "idiomatic component" ternyata preferensi estetika — terbunuh dengan murah, dan kamu tetap pegang studio yang jalan + data klien riil. Pull nyata (dev/agensi pakai & mau bayar): baru tool-nya berhak dapat roadmap, paid tier, dan kedalaman penuh.

Yang dibunuh eksplisit: all-in B · design-system wedge sebagai rencana jangka pendek · "klien service jadi user tool" · "service membangun tool".

Where the Council Clashes

2. Buat apa service-nya? Expansionist: data engine & pipeline klien. First Principles: discovery. Contrarian: "A with guilt". Putusan chairman: service sebagai mesin kas lolos; service sebagai pembangun dev tool tidak. Klien non-coder tidak akan pernah minta idiomatic TSX, runes, atau brand tokens — aturan disiplin Executor ("build teknik abi hanya saat klien memaksa") tidak mungkin ter-trigger karena klien pemicunya tidak ada.

3. Cara menguji tool. Contrarian: "tunggu sampai dev yang bayar menariknya keluar". Chairman meng-override sikap pasif itu: launch gratis ke komunitas dev itu satu post — 100k stars abi membuktikan distribusi dev itu gratis — jadikan "menunggu ditarik" operasional: pull diukur dari stars, shares, usage, dan DM "aku mau bayar".

Blind Spots the Council Caught
Caught in peer review

• Pasar service-nya juga belum tervalidasi. Nol user menghukum kedua jalur, bukan cuma B. Review hari-30 harus mendiagnosis motion penjualan (offer, channel, harga) — bukan membunuh tool.
• Unit economics nggak pernah dihitung. Halaman/bulan untuk survive = biaya bulanan ÷ tiket rata-rata. Contoh: Rp 5jt ÷ Rp 700k ≈ 8 halaman/bulan ≈ 2 klien/minggu. Dengan konversi DM dingin 1–5%, itu artinya ~40–200 DM/minggu. Angka ini di dinding menentukan lebih banyak daripada strategi mana pun.
• Agensi melarutkan masalah "opposite engines". Temuan terbaik review: customer dev tool yang benar bukan pemilik kafe dan bukan dev yang hadap v0/Cursor — tapi agensi & freelancer yang menerima job screenshot DAN memelihara codebase nyata. Mereka punya pain persis visi SliceUI, mereka bayar untuk tool, dan komunitas dev Indonesia penuh dengan mereka. Service membuat kamu jadi anggota populasi ini — itu jembatan yang selamat antara A dan B.
• Dua kill criteria, bukan satu. Kegagalan tool ≠ kegagalan sales. Aturan 30-hari Executor menggabungkan keduanya — dipisah sekarang.
• Gratis itu senjata distribusi dev: launch gratis = vonis teori gap dengan biaya minggu part-time.

Contrarian 🏆 — "Premisnya salah. Ini A dengan rasa bersalah."

The fatal flaw is the premise: "NO incumbent produces idiomatic component code." v0's entire product is Next.js/React components with shadcn — your own git history has "Lovable update" commits from a tool that does exactly this. What's actually left of the gap — "drops straight into your existing project" — requires repo context, which is the domain of Cursor and Claude Code, held by the best-funded labs, and shrinking every model cycle. Your moat would be a prompt plus three techniques from an MIT repo 100k people have read, running on Gemini free tier at 15 RPM. The self-check loop alone burns 3-5 vision calls per generation. That's not a moat; that's a demo. Second flaw: "idiomatic output" quality rides on Gemini 2.0 Flash and Groq's llama-4-scout. Developers are the pickiest users alive — they'll compare against v0 once and never return. Third flaw: C is not a path from A to B — it's A with guilt. Non-coder landing pages need standalone pretty HTML; the dev tool needs idiomatic in-repo components. Opposite engines. What I'd do: A, honestly — freelance studio with AI-assisted workflow, drop the hybrid story. Timebox 90 days, measure client acquisition. Touch B only when a paying developer pulls it out of you — demand, not reverse-engineered gap theory.

First Principles — "Bukan memilih produk — memilih customer. Tool harus earn the right to exist."

You're not choosing between products — you're choosing between customers, and only one group has any evidence it pays. Two facts survive: he needs cash in weeks, and he has zero distribution. A dev tool's fatal dependency isn't engineering time; it's distribution. Screenshot-to-idiomatic-code is a feature, not a company: abi is free with 100k stars, v0 is Vercel-backed, model labs absorb this into the prompt box. The service's bottleneck is outreach — fully controlled, converts in days. So the real question: what is the cheapest experiment producing revenue evidence? The service. Run it with SliceUI as the production tool, not an afterthought — the service doubles as discovery. Every client engagement tells you what the tool must do next. If after 20 landing pages clients ask "can I edit this myself inside my project," that's dev-tool demand, validated by people who already pay. If that signal never appears, the "idiomatic code gap" was a developer's aesthetic preference — killed before it cost six months of morale. That's C as an evidence strategy, not a funding strategy.

Expansionist — "Service = data engine. Design-system block = crown jewel."

Do C — but kill the framing that services "fund" the tool. Services are your data engine. 1) The design-system block is the crown jewel: "paste your tokens, get conformant code" is the wedge into every Indonesian product team and agency maintaining a design system. Nobody — v0, Lovable, abi — owns design-system conformance. Once a team's tokens live in your tool, churn drops to zero. 2) Every service client is proprietary training signal: paid to build the eval set and prompt hardening competitors don't have. 3) Targeted element edits is the hidden expansion path — UI pair-programming for existing codebases. Generation is crowded; editing my actual component tree is empty. Concretely: 90 days of service, every job through your pipeline; sell speed ("2-hour delivery"). Months 4–6: ship the dev tool to the design-system niche; flip best service clients into first paying users. Exit holding an agency AND a product with a moat. Option A alone exits holding a job. [Peer review: bakery clients don't have design tokens — the wedge customer isn't the service customer; the wedge itself is unvalidated; Locofy/Anima/Figma already occupy it.]

Outsider — "Gap itu hipotesi, bukan gap. Rekening bankmu sudah memutuskan."

You "found a real product gap" by reading a competitor's source code, then confirmed it with zero customers. That's not a gap — that's a hypothesis. v0, Cursor, and Claude already generate component code on demand; your claim that none do it well enough is verified entirely by your own inspection. Maybe you're right — but a broke person betting months of unpaid labor on their own technical reading of a market is the classic trap. Option B is the fun, intellectually satisfying option — exactly why it's dangerous with no runway. Your bank account already decided this: A and C both start with selling services; the only question is whether you give yourself permission to keep tinkering. Fine — keep it, but reframe it: the tool's first customer is you. Every landing-page job is a paid test — does SliceUI actually make you faster? If after ten projects it's genuinely 3x, you have evidence and maybe a product. If you keep hand-fixing the output, you just saved yourself months. And this entire question is written in a dialect only developers speak — "idiomatic," "runes," "design-system aware." The people paying you next month don't know those words exist. Go where the money already speaks your customer's language; let the tool earn its promotion.

Executor — "C versi tidak sopan: Monday plan + kill criteria."

The only option with a Monday morning attached is C — but not the polite version. B is out on contact: no first step produces rupiah. Monday: 1) Deploy to Vercel — one day, not a project. 2) Make 3 demo pages with the tool itself — bakery, clinic, wedding vendor. Fixed outputs = portfolio AND QA suite; you learn where the tool breaks, free. 3) One-page site: demos, WhatsApp, QRIS. Delete auth/persistence/Redis from the todo — none needed to get paid. 4) 20 Instagram DMs + 10 WhatsApp messages with before/after images — day-one sales. Discipline: abi's techniques get built ONLY when a client job forces them. Client hands brand tokens → build the design-system block on their paid project. Slicing ten similar pages → the self-check loop earns a weekend. Hard rule: 4 days client work, 1 day tool. Deadline: zero paying clients in 30 days kills the dev-tool ambition — learned for a month instead of six. [Peer review: the "when a client forces it" trigger can never fire — bakery/clinic clients never demand idiomatic TSX or brand tokens.]
