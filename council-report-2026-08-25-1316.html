The Question

Sudah memutuskan arah "corrected-C" (jasa landing page ke non-coder Indonesia + tool screenshot→code ke agensi/global). Tool bernama "SliceUI". Usulan nama baru "Paper Pixel". Pertanyaan: arsitektur penamaan mana yang benar agar user tidak bingung — (a) Paper Pixel semua, (b) SliceUI semua, (c) dua brand terpisah, (d) satu payung brand + nama produk internal?

The One Thing to Do First

Cek ketersediaan & daftarkan domain + handle service hari ini — sebelum bicara nama lagi. Shortlist 2–3 nama Bahasa polos (bukan "Paper Pixel"), cek domain (.id/.com) + WhatsApp Business + Instagram sekarang, beli yang kosong. Ketersediaan — bukan metafora — satu-satunya bagian keputusan ini yang irreversible dan murah diselesaikan. Nama tool tidak butuh tindakan apa pun minggu ini.

The Recommendation

Simpan "SliceUI" untuk tool. Beri service nama Bahasa-first yang polos. Jangan pakai "Paper Pixel" di mana pun yang dilihat audiens yang bayar.

Tool: biarkan SliceUI apa adanya. Sudah ada (repo, domain, nama), dan "slice" = kata yang dipahami agensi global. Nol biaya rename, nol keputusan hari ini. Tinjau ulang hanya jika dia punya user.

Service: buang metaforanya. Pilih nama yang bisa diucapkan pemilik klinik ke tetangga & diketik di HP — selevel "WebsiteJadi" / "LamanKilat" = "websitemu, jadi". Syarat: Bahasa-friendly, deskriptif (website untuk bisnis), mudah diketik, dan tersedia di .id + WhatsApp Business + Instagram.

Legal seam: QRIS itu workstream sendiri (entitas terdaftar, akun merchant) — jangan disembunyikan di dalam keputusan nama.

Tanpa umbrella: satu founder, dua nama, tanpa aset brand bersama. Kalau service nanti membesar jadi brand studio, nama itu yang tumbuh jadi payung dengan sendirinya.

Where the Council Agrees
High confidence — 5/5 & 4/5

• 5/5 konvergen ke (c): dua brand terpisah. Satu founder = dua perusahaan — jasa Bahasa untuk pemilik bakery/klinik, dan tool English untuk agensi global. Audiens tidak pernah overlap (bahasa, intensi, channel) → satu nama melayani keduanya = gagal; umbrella (d) = over-engineering di budget nol.
• 4/5 setuju "Paper Pixel" salah untuk service. "Pixel" itu loanword teknis yang tidak bisa diketik/dipahami pemilik bakery Bandung; metaforanya cuma nyambung bagi yang sudah tahu workflow screenshot-to-code (curse of knowledge). Terbaca sebagai toko alat tulis, bukan "saya perlu website yang terima QRIS."
• Mesin kas harus bawa nama polos & terpercaya; tool boleh bawa nama teknis deskriptif.

Where the Council Clashes
One dissent, overruled

Dissent tunggal: Expansionist — memberi "Paper Pixel" ke service karena "paper → pixel" literal mendeskripsikan serah scribble → dapat website, dan "English reads premium to Indonesian SMBs." Metaforanya cerdas, tapi terbalik dalam praktik: "English reads premium" itu asumsi tanpa bukti, dan mengabaikan constraint eksplisit (mudah diketik/diingat/diucap via WhatsApp). Kelima reviewer independen menandainya sebagai blind spot terbesar. Council menolaknya.

Ketegangan lunak (sequencing, bukan kontradiksi): Contrarian bilang "satu nama untuk mesin kas, rename tool nanti kalau dia pantas" — sementara First Principles & Review 5 bilang "nama tool tidak perlu diputuskan sekarang sama sekali" (nol user, nol equity, nol biaya rename).

Blind Spots the Council Caught
Caught in peer review — more important than the naming

1. Nama tidak load-bearing untuk discovery. SMB Indonesia menemukan jasa lewat WhatsApp, Instagram, Google Maps, dan referral — bukan search brand. Offer & channel yang kerja; nama itu sinyal trust, bukan sumber trafik. Ini mengempiskan seluruh kecemasan "nama mana yang menang".
2. QRIS itu legal seam, bukan tombol. Terima QRIS butuh entitas merchant Indonesia terdaftar. Solo dev tidak bisa sekadar "tambah tombol QRIS" — ini tugas pembentukan badan usaha, bukan tugas UI, dan ada di jalur kritis mesin kas benar-benar menerima uang.
3. Tool mereproduksi desain ber-hak-cipta. Screenshot-to-code menyalin UI yang ada → liability IP/takedown. Entitas hukum bersama akan membiarkan risiko itu bocor ke income service yang stabil — alasan lagi untuk memisahkan keduanya secara legal, bukan cuma brand.
4. Tidak ada yang benar-benar cek ketersediaan. Domain (.id/.com), handle sosial, nama WhatsApp Business = constraint eksplisit yang di-assert semua advisor tapi tidak ada yang cek. Ketersediaan, bukan puisi, yang jadi gate sebenarnya.

Advisor Full Responses (collapse)
Executor 🏆 — "Jangan overthink. Nama = biaya tersamar; cek domain & beli hari ini."

Don't overthink this. Naming is a cost question in disguise; the cheapest path is (c) — not because it's strategically right, but because it costs nothing extra. You already built SliceUI — domain, repo, name exist. Renaming to Paper Pixel is pure cost with zero payoff. Two audiences = why ONE name fails: "Paper Pixel" is an English metaphor a Bandung bakery owner can't type/spell/remember; "SliceUI" is tech-gibberish to a clinic owner paying QRIS. Umbrella is a luxury for when you have equity — you have none. Monday: 1) Keep SliceUI as tool name — touch nothing. 2) Build ONE landing page for the service under a name a non-coder Indonesian can type and say — drop the poetry, pick plain Bahasa-native like "WebsiteJadi.id" or "LamanKilat", check domain, buy today. 3) QRIS button + WhatsApp CTA on that one page. Ship it. The brand you sell money with gets the plain, typable name.

Contrarian — "Nama yang membayar harus lokal; 'Paper Pixel' meracuni bisnis yang mendanaimu."

The fatal flaw isn't the architecture — it's that "Paper Pixel" is the wrong name for the only audience that actually pays you. Indonesian bakery/clinic owners speak Bahasa, don't know what a "pixel" is, and won't type "paperpixel.id" reliably. Option (a) — "Paper Pixel for everything" — is worst: it poisons the business that funds you. Option (d) spreads a bad name wider. Option (c) is conceptually right but you're solo with zero budget/equity: two brands across two languages = two domains, logos, handles, SEO efforts, done badly. Name the thing that pays: the service carries a local, trustworthy, Bahasa-friendly name a bakery owner can repeat over WhatsApp. The tool is speculative — give it a descriptive technical name (SliceUI is fine). One name for the cash engine now; rename the tool later only if it earns it.

First Principles — "Salah tanya. Kamu punya dua perusahaan, bukan dua produk."

There is no "naming architecture" to choose — you don't have one company with two products; you have two companies sharing a founder. "Architecture" is a word for P&G. What are you solving? A Bahasa-language landing-page service sold to Indonesian bakery/clinic owners, and an English-language screenshot-to-code tool for global agencies. These audiences never overlap — not in language, purchase intent, or discovery channel. So the "confusion" you fear is imaginary: confusion requires a real human seeing both names. No bakery owner will ever see your tool; no agency will ever see your service. The service gets a Bahasa name; "Paper Pixel" is an English metaphor meaning nothing to a clinic owner — actively worse than generic. The tool keeps SliceUI or becomes Paper Pixel; either works for English devs. One founder, two names, no shared umbrella. Your real problem is spending decision-energy on the side with no revenue.

Outsider — "Aku nggak ngerti dua-duanya — dan customer-mu juga nggak."

I don't understand either name from its own description, and neither will your customers. "SliceUI" is insider jargon — "slicing" is a thing only frontend people say; a bakery owner in Bandung hears "slice" and thinks pre-cut cake. "Paper Pixel" is worse: the metaphor only makes sense to someone who already knows the screenshot-to-code workflow — curse of knowledge in pure form. To an outsider, "Paper Pixel" reads as a stationery store or print shop. You'd rename the one clear, functional thing ("UI" signals software) into something signaling paper goods. So (c) — two brands — but not with your proposed names. The two customers have opposite mental models: "I need a website for my shop" vs "I need to convert a design to code faster." Keep "SliceUI" for the tool — global agencies understand "slice," it's their word. Give the service a Bahasa-first, descriptive name about websites for businesses. Don't hide your cash engine behind a metaphor only you understand.

Expansionist ❌ — "Paper Pixel = service, SliceUI = tool." (Ditolak 5/5 reviewer)

(c) — but with deliberate assignment: "Paper Pixel" is the service, "SliceUI" stays the tool. The metaphor "paper design → finished pixels" describes the SERVICE, not the tool — a bakery owner hands you a scribble and gets a website, literally. "Slice" already says screenshot-to-code better. The Indonesian service is the compounding asset; productize it — "Paper Pixel" becomes a studio brand a non-coder can say to a neighbor. English words read premium to Indonesian SMBs — a feature, not a bug. SliceUI, descriptive and generic, is fine for developers. Two clean brands, separately optimizable. The umbrella — if it ever earns one — is what "Paper Pixel" grows into. [Peer review, unanimous: assigns "Paper Pixel" to the paying audience that can't type/understand "pixel"; "English reads premium" is asserted not evidenced; commits the exact curse-of-knowledge error.]
