## Original Question (user)

"oke kita sudah mempunyai tujuan C tadi dan perlu penamaan lagi untuk jasa, agar kedepan nya user tidak bingung, product kita ini bagus nya diberi nama apa, paper pixel untuk semua aspek atau slice ui, atau combine inside"

## Framed Question

An Indonesian solo frontend developer settled on "corrected C": sell landing-page SERVICES to Indonesian non-coders (bakery/clinic, Bahasa, QRIS/WhatsApp) now as cash engine, while separately testing a screenshot-to-code TOOL to global agencies/freelancers via free launch later. Tool is currently "SliceUI" (descriptive-generic). Developer proposes "Paper Pixel" (paper design → finished pixels). Question: which naming architecture avoids user confusion — (a) Paper Pixel for everything, (b) SliceUI for everything, (c) two separate brands (SliceUI=tool, Paper Pixel=service), (d) one umbrella brand + internal product. Constraints: solo, minimal budget, zero brand equity, two audiences, easy to type/remember/available.

---

## Advisor Responses

### The Contrarian
The fatal flaw isn't the naming architecture — it's that "Paper Pixel" is the wrong name for the only audience that actually pays you. Your cash engine is Indonesian bakery and clinic owners. They speak Bahasa, don't know what a "pixel" is, and won't type "paperpixel.id" reliably. "Pixel" is a technical loanword; the metaphor means nothing to someone who just wants a website that takes QRIS. Option (a) — "Paper Pixel for everything" — is worst: it poisons the business that funds you. Option (d) spreads a bad name wider and doubles brand assets you can't afford. Option (c) is conceptually right but you're solo with zero budget/equity: two brands across two languages = two domains, logos, handles, SEO efforts — done badly, so neither gets traction. Name the thing that pays: the service should carry a local, trustworthy, Bahasa-friendly name a bakery owner can repeat over WhatsApp. The tool is speculative — give it a descriptive technical name (SliceUI is fine). Reject "Paper Pixel" as umbrella, reject (a), don't pretend to sustain (c) or (d). One name for the cash engine now; rename the tool later only if it earns it.

### The First Principles Thinker
You're asking the wrong question. There is no "naming architecture" to choose, because you don't have one company with two products — you have two companies sharing a founder. "Architecture" is a word for P&G, not a solo dev with zero budget. What are you solving? A Bahasa-language landing-page service sold to Indonesian bakery/clinic owners, and an English-language screenshot-to-code tool for global agencies. These audiences never overlap — not in language, purchase intent, or discovery channel. So the "confusion" you fear is imaginary: confusion requires a real human seeing both names. No bakery owner will ever see your tool; no agency will ever see your service. The service gets a Bahasa name (that's what your customer types into Google); "Paper Pixel" is an English metaphor meaning nothing to a clinic owner — actively worse than generic. The tool keeps SliceUI or becomes Paper Pixel; either works for English devs. One founder, two names, no shared umbrella. Your real problem is spending decision-energy on the side with no revenue.

### The Expansionist
(c) — but with deliberate assignment: "Paper Pixel" is the service, "SliceUI" stays the tool. The metaphor "paper design → finished pixels" describes the SERVICE, not the tool. A bakery owner hands you a scribble or screenshot and gets a website — that's paper-to-pixel, literally. "Slice" already says screenshot-to-code better. You almost built the perfect name for the wrong side. The real upside: the Indonesian service is the compounding asset. Productize it — "Paper Pixel" becomes a studio brand a non-coder can say to a neighbor, remember, trust. Landing pages today, retainers, then "website + WhatsApp + QRIS" bundles, then a mini-agency. English words read premium to Indonesian SMBs — a feature, not a bug. Don't contaminate it with a dev tool. SliceUI, descriptive and generic, is fine for developers — zero equity, zero ceiling. One umbrella brand is premature over-engineering on zero budget. Two clean brands, separately optimizable. The umbrella — if it ever earns one — is what "Paper Pixel" grows into.

### The Outsider
I don't understand either name from its own description, and neither will your customers. "SliceUI" is insider jargon — "slicing" is a thing only frontend people say; a bakery owner in Bandung hears "slice" and thinks pre-cut cake. "Paper Pixel" is worse: the metaphor only makes sense to someone who already knows the screenshot-to-code workflow — the curse of knowledge in pure form. To an outsider, "Paper Pixel" reads as a stationery store or print shop. You'd rename the one clear, functional thing ("UI" at least signals software) into something signaling paper goods. So the architecture is (c) — two brands — but not with your proposed names. The two customers have opposite mental models: "I need a website for my shop" vs "I need to convert a design to code faster." Keep "SliceUI" for the tool — global agencies actually understand "slice," it's their word. Give the service a Bahasa-first, descriptive name about websites for businesses — something a clinic owner can say to a neighbor. Don't hide your cash engine behind a metaphor only you understand.

### The Executor
Don't overthink this. Naming is a cost question in disguise; the cheapest path is (c) — not because it's strategically right, but because it costs nothing extra. You already built SliceUI — domain, repo, name exist. Renaming to Paper Pixel is pure cost with zero payoff: new domain, handles, logo, a week you should spend selling. Two audiences = why ONE name (a or b) fails: "Paper Pixel" is an English metaphor a Bandung bakery owner can't type/spell/remember; "SliceUI" is tech-gibberish to a clinic owner paying QRIS. Umbrella (d) is a luxury for when you have equity — you have none. Monday morning: 1) Keep SliceUI as the tool name — touch nothing. 2) Build ONE landing page for the service under a name a non-coder Indonesian can type and say. Drop the "Paper Pixel" poetry — pick plain and Bahasa-native like "WebsiteJadi.id" or "LamanKilat" — check domain, buy today. 3) QRIS button + WhatsApp CTA on that one page. Ship it. The brand you sell money with gets the plain, typable name.

## Chairman Synthesis

### Where the Council Agrees
- **5/5 converge on (c): two separate brands.** One founder = two companies (Bahasa service for Indonesian SMBs; English tool for global agencies). Audiences never overlap in language, intent, or channel → a single shared name serves neither; an umbrella (d) is over-engineering for zero budget/equity.
- **4/5 agree "Paper Pixel" is wrong for the service.** "Pixel" is a technical loanword a Bandung bakery owner can't type or understand; the metaphor only decodes for someone who already knows screenshot-to-code (curse of knowledge). Reads as stationery/print-shop. The cash engine must carry a plain, trustworthy, Bahasa-native name; the tool can keep a descriptive technical name.

### Where the Council Clashes
- **The lone dissent: Expansionist** — giving "Paper Pixel" to the service because "paper → pixel" literally describes handing over a scribble and getting a website, and "English reads premium to Indonesian SMBs." Clever metaphor, exactly backwards in practice; "English reads premium" is asserted not evidenced; ignores the explicit easy-to-type constraint. All five reviewers flagged it as the biggest blind spot. Council overrules it.
- **Soft sequencing tension (not contradiction):** Contrarian says "one name for the cash engine, rename the tool later if it earns it"; First Principles + Review 5 go further — "the tool name needn't be decided at all right now" (zero users/equity, zero rename cost).

### Blind Spots the Council Caught
1. **The name is not load-bearing for discovery.** Indonesian SMBs find providers via WhatsApp, Instagram, Google Maps, referral — not brand search. Offer and channel do the work; the name is a trust signal, not a traffic source. This deflates the entire "which name wins" anxiety.
2. **QRIS is a legal seam, not a button.** Accepting QRIS requires a registered Indonesian merchant entity — a business-formation task, not a UI task; it's on the critical path to the cash engine actually receiving money.
3. **The tool reproduces copyrighted designs.** Screenshot-to-code copies existing UIs → IP/takedown liability; a shared legal entity would leak that risk onto the stable service income — another reason to separate cleanly (legally, not just brand-wise).
4. **Nobody verified availability.** Domain (.id/.com), social handles, WhatsApp Business name — an explicit constraint everyone asserted but no one checked. Availability, not poetry, is the real gate.
5. **The shared surface is the founder, not a brand.** A coherent "I build software and sites for businesses" story across a GitHub/portfolio profile matters more than any logo.

### The Recommendation
**Keep SliceUI for the tool. Give the service a plain, Bahasa-first name. Do not use "Paper Pixel" anywhere the paying audience sees it.**

- **Tool:** Leave SliceUI exactly as-is (repo, domain, name exist; "slice" is understood by global agencies). Zero rename cost, zero decision today. Revisit only if it earns users.
- **Service:** Drop the metaphor. Pick a name a clinic owner can say to a neighbor and type into a phone — on the order of "WebsiteJadi" / "LamanKilat" = "your website, done." Requirements: Bahasa-friendly, descriptive (websites for businesses), typeable, available across .id domain + WhatsApp Business + Instagram.
- **Legal seam:** Treat QRIS as its own workstream (registered entity, merchant account) — don't hide it inside a naming decision.
- **No umbrella:** One founder, two names, no shared brand asset. If the service compounds into a studio brand later, that name grows into the umbrella on its own.

### The One Thing to Do First
**Check availability and register the service domain and handles today — before another word about names.** Shortlist two or three plain Bahasa names (not "Paper Pixel"), run them through domain (.id/.com), WhatsApp Business, and Instagram availability right now, and buy the one that's free. Availability — not the metaphor — is the only part of this decision that is both irreversible and cheap to resolve. The tool name needs no action at all this week.
